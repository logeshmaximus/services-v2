Why the age matters electrically

A building from 1906 was wired, if it was wired at all, for lighting. Everything since — the refrigerator, the window units, the home office, the induction range, the car — has been added to a service that was never designed for it. That is the single thread running through most Chicago electrical work.

Township by township

West Chicago is the oldest at 83% pre-1940 across 65,664 properties. Lake View follows at 71% across 23,046. Jefferson township — Portage Park, Jefferson Park, Irving Park — is younger at 60% pre-1940 but far larger at 112,923 properties, and its median year of 1927 puts most of it in the bungalow era. Rogers Park is the youngest of the north-side townships at a 1938 median.

What that means room by room

188,389 properties citywide are two-storey and 154,926 single-storey; 324,305 have full basements, which is where the panel usually lives and where access is usually straightforward. 107,336 properties have no garage at all — relevant the moment an EV charger enters the conversation. And 335,769 use a warm-air furnace against 98,849 on hot water or steam, which decides what kind of equipment circuits exist already.

Where we work most

Ward 1 (129 permits), Ward 32 (85), Ward 26 (72), Ward 47 (68) and Ward 27 (63) lead our record — the north and northwest sides, consistent with a shop on N Lincoln Ave.

The citywide picture

Chicago issued 20,187 electrical permits in 24 months. Lighting leads at 9,748, outlets and receptacles at 9,306, service work at 6,672, panels at 5,219 and solar at 4,033. At the other end, rewires ran to 28 permits and ceiling fans to 3 — a useful corrective to any contractor telling you a whole-house rewire is routine.

One honest caveat

Permit values are the reported construction value of the whole project, which often includes trades other than ours. They are a good guide to the scale of a job and a poor guide to a contractor's revenue. We quote from scope, not from permit data.
